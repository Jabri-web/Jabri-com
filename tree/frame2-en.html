The Tree and the Shadow

A reproducible geometric and numerical framework: 22.5° angle proof, interactive simulation, graph, and initial field measurements table.

Author: Eng. Abdulla M. N. Al-Jabri Sho'ub — Sana'a — Yemen
Frame 1 DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23540283 Frame 2 DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23540796 OSF DOI: 10.17605/OSF.IO/YC9MB

This page distinguishes between the exact mathematical proof, exploratory field data, and hypotheses that still require independent testing.

1. Exact Mathematical Result

If the angle θ is measured from the vertical direction, then the sun's elevation angle from the horizon is α = 90° − θ. For a sun ray making an elevation angle α with a horizontal ground, a right-angled triangle yields that the ratio of horizontal shadow to vertical height is:

S / H = cot(α) = tan(θ)

At θ = 22.5°, α = 67.5°, and therefore:

tan(22.5°) = tan(45° / 2) = √2 − 1 ≈ 0.4142135624

Half-Angle Proof

Let x = tan(22.5°). Since tan(45°) = 1, from the double-angle formula:

1 = tan(45°) = 2x / (1 − x²)

Rearranging gives x² + 2x − 1 = 0. Solving the quadratic equation:

x = −1 ± √2

Since 22.5° is an acute angle, x must be positive, therefore:

x = √2 − 1
What is proven? The exact trigonometric value √2−1, and the geometric shadow relationship under the assumptions of a horizontal surface, a straight light ray, and a correct angle measurement. This alone does not prove that a field-measured tree crown ratio must equal this value.

Statistical Note

The displayed 90% confidence interval is a t-interval for the sample mean (n=5, degrees of freedom 4). The TOST equivalence test depends on the equivalence margin Δ defined by the researcher in advance; passing the test does not imply exact equality or a general natural law. The previous p-value (0.000801) is treated here as a computational result from the previous analysis, provided the same sample, margin, and procedures are used, and should be recalculated from the data file/software before final publication.

6. Proof Formulation and Final Conclusion

  1. Model assumptions: A vertical object of height H above a horizontal ground, and a light ray making an elevation angle α with the horizon.
  2. From the right triangle: tan(α) = H/S, hence S/H = 1/tan(α).
  3. Angle definition from vertical: θ = 90°−α, therefore cot(α) = tan(θ), yielding S/H = tan(θ).
  4. At θ = 22.5°: The theoretical shadow-to-height ratio is √2−1 ≈ 0.41421356.
  5. Data check: The mean field ratio R ≈ 0.41297 is numerically close to the theoretical value, but R = (H−h)/H is not S/H unless the measurement proves that H−h is a horizontal shadow length; therefore, this sample is not a direct test of the shadow law.
Proven result: S/H = tan(θ), and at θ=22.5° it equals √2−1.
Controlled Conclusion: The geometric proof is exact. The mango data is exploratory and shows a numerical convergence between the mean R and the value √2−1, not a proof that nature imposes this ratio on trees. There is no proof in this page of a link to the Riemann hypothesis or to a broader cosmic law.

7. Reproducibility Steps and Links

  • Save the file as frame2-en.html and open it in a modern browser; no libraries or internet connection required.
  • Adjust H and θ in the simulation, and verify that S = H × tan(θ).
  • To convert the study into a direct field test, measure the object height H and horizontal shadow length S at the same time, record the sun angle, and repeat measurements at different times, locations, and objects.
  • Keep raw data, measurement units, uncertainty estimation method, and a pre-specified analysis plan before collecting new data.
