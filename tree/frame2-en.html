The Tree and the Shadow

A geometric and numerical frame, reproducible in the browser: proof of the 22.5° angle, an interactive simulation, a bar chart, the preliminary measurement table, and a TOST equivalence test computed in-page with automated verification of the t distribution.

Author: Abdulla Mohammed Nasser Al-Jabri Shu'ub — Sana'a — Yemen

The frame begins with an exact trigonometric result, then builds an interactive simulation on it, then presents preliminary field measurements that are examined exploratorily against a pre-specified equivalence margin.

Opening — Problem, Achievement, Type of Evidence

Problem

How can an exact geometric relationship (tan 22.5° = √2−1) be linked to measurable field observations, and what is actually inferred from a numerical proximity between a crown/height ratio in a small sample and the theoretical value?

Achievement

An exact trigonometric proof of tan(22.5°) = √2−1, the shadow ratio relation S/H = tan(θ) within the geometric model, an interactive simulation, preliminary measurements of five mango trees, and a TOST equivalence test reproducible within the page with visible reference tests.

Type of Evidence

An exact mathematical proof for the trigonometric result. A geometric model with specified conditions (horizontal ground, straight light ray) for the shadow relation. Exploratory field data (n=5) showing numerical proximity, not proof. This frame makes no proven claim of a connection to the Riemann hypothesis.

1. The Exact Mathematical Result

If the angle θ is measured from the vertical, then the sun's elevation angle above the horizon is α = 90° − θ. For a solar ray making elevation angle α with a horizontal ground, a right triangle yields that the ratio of the horizontal shadow to the vertical height is:

S / H = cot(α) = tan(θ)

At θ = 22.5°, α = 67.5°, and therefore:

tan(22.5°) = tan(45° / 2) = √2 − 1 ≈ 0.4142135624

Half-angle proof

Let x = tan(22.5°). Since tan(45°) = 1, the double-angle formula gives:

1 = tan(45°) = 2x / (1 − x²)

Rearranging gives x² + 2x − 1 = 0. Solving the quadratic:

x = −1 ± √2

Since 22.5° is an acute angle, x is positive, therefore:

x = √2 − 1
What has been proved? The exact trigonometric value √2−1, and the geometric shadow relation under the assumptions of horizontal ground, a straight light ray, and a correctly measured angle. This alone does not prove that a crown ratio measured in the field must equal this value.

Crucial distinction between the two models:
  • Geometric model (Section 2): the ratio S/H = tan(θ) applies to a horizontal shadow, under assumptions of horizontal ground, straight ray, and accurate angle measurement.
  • Field data (Section 3): the ratio R = (H−h)/H is a crown/height ratio, not a shadow length.

A successful TOST comparing mean R to √2−1 does not prove the shadow law S/H = tan(θ), because the two are different quantities. The honest phrasing is: "mean crown/height ratio in this sample is numerically close to √2−1 within the specified margin", without claiming causality or generalizing to mango trees at large.

TOST equivalence test — computed from the five data points

The two-one-sided equivalence test at α = 0.05 corresponds to a 90% confidence interval for the mean lying entirely inside [μtarget−Δ, μtarget+Δ]. The margin Δ must be specified before data collection; changing it after seeing the result invalidates the test's confirmatory meaning.

p₁ is the one-sided test of the lower bound (H₀: μ ≤ μtarget−Δ); p₂ is the one-sided test of the upper bound (H₀: μ ≥ μtarget+Δ); p_TOST = max(p₁, p₂). Equivalence at α=0.05 is concluded when p_TOST < 0.05. Note that n=5 is small, statistical power is limited, and the conclusion depends on the chosen margin.

Statistical note

The displayed 90% CI is the t-based interval for the sample mean (n=5, df=4). The TOST in Section 3 is computed from the same five data points and reports the margin, degrees of freedom, p₁, p₂, and p_TOST. Passing the test does not amount to proving exact equality or a general natural law. The reference tests of the t distribution (Section 3) show whether the displayed p-values can be trusted before relying on them.

6. Proof Formulation and Final Conclusion

  1. Model assumptions: a vertical object of height H above horizontal ground, with a light ray making elevation angle α with the horizon.
  2. From the right triangle: tan(α) = H/S, hence S/H = 1/tan(α).
  3. Angle defined from the vertical: θ = 90°−α, therefore cot(α) = tan(θ), yielding S/H = tan(θ).
  4. At θ = 22.5°: the theoretical shadow-to-height ratio is √2−1 ≈ 0.41421356.
  5. Data check: the field mean R ≈ 0.41297 is numerically close to the theoretical value, but R = (H−h)/H is not S/H unless the measurement established that H−h is the horizontal shadow length; therefore this sample is not a direct test of the shadow law.
Proven result: S/H = tan(θ), and at θ=22.5° it equals √2−1.
Controlled conclusion: the geometric proof is exact. The mango data are exploratory and show numerical proximity between mean R and √2−1, not a demonstration that nature imposes this ratio on trees. Nothing on this page proves a connection to the Riemann hypothesis or to a broader cosmic law.

7. Reproduction Steps and Links

  • Save the file as frame2-en.html and open in a modern browser. Calculations, plots, and CSV export work offline. The "Download PDF" button requires loading html2pdf.js from CDN on first use, or hosting it locally.
  • Change H and θ in the simulation and verify that S = H × tan(θ). Invalid inputs are flagged as you type and corrected when the field loses focus.
  • Change the margin Δ in Section 3 to see its effect on the TOST outcome; the margin should be fixed before data collection.
  • Review the reference t distribution tests in Section 3 before relying on p_TOST.
  • To turn the study into a direct field test, measure the object height H and the horizontal shadow length S at the same time, record the sun angle, and repeat across times, sites, and objects.
  • Keep the raw data, units, uncertainty estimation method, and the analysis plan fixed before collecting new data.
Exported CSV structure

The file begins with #-comment metadata identifying the frame, author, DOI, collection date and site, units, and a summary of descriptive statistics, followed by the column header row:

tree, H_m, h_m, C_m, R, target_sqrt2_minus_1, difference_from_target

Columns tree, H_m, h_m are the measured data; C_m, R, difference_from_target are derived; target_sqrt2_minus_1 is a theoretical constant. The file does not contain additional raw measurements or a record of instruments and uncertainty, which must be added before use in a new analysis.
