\begin{abstract}
We study a one-parameter family of real functions \(g(u; x_p)\) on \((0, 2\pi)\) defined by
\[
g(u; x_p) = \left(\frac{u}{2\pi}\right)^{5} \ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) e^{-2\pi/(x_p u)},
\]
with \(x_p > 0\). The family is motivated by, but strictly independent of, the arithmetic of the second non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function; the specific value \(x_p = 21\) is a modelling choice.

We establish four results. First, an exact algebraic identity \(H_g(gY) = g(-Y'')\) relating the standard second-derivative operator acting on \(Y\) to a first-order-corrected operator \(H_g = -\partial_u^2 + 2p\partial_u + (p'-p^2)\), where \(p = g'/g\). Second, an exact symmetry argument showing that a matrix element of the operator \(D(u) = \cos(u/2)\) between normalized states vanishes in a reflection-symmetric reference model. Third, a numerical demonstration that the asymmetry of \(g\) in \((0, 2\pi)\) breaks this vanishing: for \(x_p = 21\), the specified transition operator, and the specified unweighted \(L^2\) normalization, composite Simpson quadrature reproducibly yields \(R_{13/12} \approx 0.6035155248\). Fourth, we show that \(g\) is a formal zero mode of the operator \(L = -\partial_u^2 + g''/g\), which factors as \(L = (-\partial_u - p)(\partial_u - p)\).

The results are either algebraic identities or numerical observations for the stated setup; Section~\ref{sec:scope} states the exact scope of the claims.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} algebraic gauging; Sturm-Liouville theory; symmetry-breaking; numerical integration.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Context}

A companion project of exploratory documents \cite{Frame1,Frame2} investigates elementary functions on \((0, 2\pi)\) constructed from the ordinates \(\gamma_k\) of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. The present note isolates the mathematical content of two of those documents and states it in a self-contained form suitable for external review.

The central object is the family \eqref{eq:g} below, parameterized by \(x_p > 0\). The choice \(x_p = 21 = \lfloor \gamma_2 \rfloor\), where \(\gamma_2 \approx 21.022\) is the second positive non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function, is used throughout for numerical illustrations. It is a \emph{modelling choice}; no arithmetic property of \(\gamma_2\) enters any proof.

\subsection{What is specific to this kernel}

The individual techniques used here are standard in Sturm--Liouville theory and in supersymmetric quantum mechanics \cite{Teschl,SUSYQM}. The contribution of the present note is their application to the specific kernel \eqref{eq:g}, and the following observations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Structural asymmetry.} Unlike exactly solvable problems whose potentials are reflection-symmetric about the midpoint of the interval, the effective potential \(V_{\text{eff}} = g''/g\) associated with \eqref{eq:g} has a positive fourth-order pole at one endpoint and a negative first-order pole at the other (Section~\ref{sec:asymptotics}). The reflection symmetry that would force the vanishing of certain matrix elements (Proposition~\ref{prop:symmetry}) is therefore broken.
    
    \item \textbf{Quantitative fingerprint.} For the specific kernel, parameter value, transition operator, and normalization considered here, the asymmetry produces the numerical value \(R_{13/12} \approx 0.6035155248\) (Observation~\ref{obs:numerical}). This is a numerical observation for the stated setup; no universality is claimed.
    
    \item \textbf{Formal zero mode.} The kernel \(g\) itself is a formal zero mode of the operator \(L\), giving a supersymmetric factorization (Proposition~\ref{prop:zero-mode}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope of claims}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{What is proven.} Proposition~\ref{prop:gauging} (gauging identity) is proven for any \(C^2\) function \(Y\) and any \(g \in C^2(0,2\pi)\) with \(g > 0\). Proposition~\ref{prop:symmetry} (vanishing in the reference model) is proven for any continuous, positive, reflection-symmetric weight \(h\) with finite normalization integrals. Proposition~\ref{prop:zero-mode} (formal zero mode and factorization) is proven as an algebraic identity. The endpoint asymptotics of Section~\ref{sec:asymptotics} are derived in full.

\paragraph{What is observed numerically.} For the specified kernel, parameter value, transition operator, and normalization, composite Simpson quadrature reproducibly gives \(R_{13/12} \approx 0.6035155248\) (Observation~\ref{obs:numerical}), in agreement with an independent adaptive-quadrature reference.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} No proof of, or contribution to, the Riemann Hypothesis. No spectral theorem for \(H_g\) or \(L\) on a self-adjoint realization. No cosmological or physical interpretation. No universality of \(R_{13/12}\) across alternative normalizations or transition operators. The choice \(x_p = 21\) carries no arithmetic necessity.

\section{Setup}
% ============================================================

\subsection{The kernel and its logarithmic derivative}

Throughout, \(u\) ranges over \((0, 2\pi)\) and \(x_p > 0\). We write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:g}
g(u) := \left(\frac{u}{2\pi}\right)^{5} \ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) e^{-2\pi/(x_p u)},
\end{equation}
suppressing the dependence on \(x_p\) when no confusion can arise. The logarithmic derivative is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p}
p(u) := \frac{g'(u)}{g(u)} = \frac{5}{u} - \frac{1}{u \ln(2\pi/u)} + \frac{2\pi}{x_p u^2},
\end{equation}
and its derivative is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dp}
p'(u) = -\frac{5}{u^2} + \frac{\ln(2\pi/u) - 1}{u^2 \ln^2(2\pi/u)} - \frac{4\pi}{x_p u^3}.
\end{equation}

The function \(g\) is strictly positive on \((0, 2\pi)\) and vanishes at both endpoints; its asymptotics are analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:asymptotics}. We record for later use that \(g\) decays faster than any power of \(u\) as \(u \to 0^{+}\), but near \(u = 2\pi\) it vanishes only linearly in \(t = 2\pi - u\).

\subsection{The differential operator \(H_g\)}

Let \(H_g\) denote the second-order differential operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Hg}
H_g := -\partial_u^2 + 2 p(u)\,\partial_u + p'(u) - p(u)^2,
\end{equation}
acting on \(C^2\) functions. This operator is not self-adjoint with respect to the standard inner product on \(L^2((0,2\pi))\); it carries a first-order correction \(2p\,\partial_u\), which is the origin of the gauging identity.

\subsection{States, normalization, and matrix elements}

For \(n \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\) let \(Y_n(u) := \sin(nu/2)\). Assume the weight \(g\) is such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Nn}
N_n := \int_0^{2\pi} g(u)^2 Y_n(u)^2 \, du \in (0, \infty),
\end{equation}
which holds for \(g\) as in \eqref{eq:g}. Define the normalized states
\begin{equation}
\Psi_n(u) := \frac{g(u) Y_n(u)}{\sqrt{N_n}}.
\end{equation}
For the transition operator \(D(u) := \cos(u/2)\), define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Amn}
\mathcal{A}_{mn} := \int_0^{2\pi} \Psi_m(u) D(u) \Psi_n(u) \, du,
\end{equation}
and the dimensionless ratio
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:R}
R_{13/12} := \frac{|\mathcal{A}_{13}|^{2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{12}|^{2}}.
\end{equation}

The \emph{reference model} replaces \(g\) by a weight \(h \in C^0(0,2\pi)\) satisfying \(h(2\pi - u) = h(u)\) and \(h > 0\) on \((0,2\pi)\), with normalization integrals
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tilde-Nn}
0 < \tilde{N}_n := \int_0^{2\pi} h(u)^2 Y_n(u)^2 \, du < \infty, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3.
\end{equation}

% ============================================================
\section{The gauging identity}
\label{sec:gauging}
% ============================================================

\begin{proposition}[Gauging identity]
\label{prop:gauging}
Let \(g \in C^2(0, 2\pi)\) with \(g(u) > 0\) on \((0, 2\pi)\), and define \(p := g'/g\). Let \(H_g\) be the operator \eqref{eq:Hg}. Then for every \(Y \in C^2(0, 2\pi)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gauging}
H_g(g Y) = -g\,Y''.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We compute
\begin{align*}
H_g(gY) &= -\partial_u^2(gY) + 2 p\,\partial_u(gY) + (p'-p^2)(gY) \\
&= -(g'' Y + 2 g' Y' + g Y'') + 2\frac{g'}{g}(g' Y + g Y') + (p'-p^2) g Y.
\end{align*}
The \(g'Y'\) terms cancel. Using \(p' = g''/g - p^2\), we have \(p'-p^2 = g''/g - 2p^2\), hence
\begin{align*}
H_g(gY) &= -g'' Y - g Y'' + 2 p^2 g Y + \left(\frac{g''}{g} - 2 p^2\right) g Y \\
&= -g'' Y - g Y'' + 2 p^2 g Y + g'' Y - 2 p^2 g Y = -g Y''.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:eigen}
Suppose \(Y \in C^2(0, 2\pi)\) satisfies \(-Y'' = \lambda Y\) for some \(\lambda \in \mathbb{R}\). Then \(\psi := g Y\) satisfies \(H_g \psi = \lambda \psi\) in \((0, 2\pi)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:gauging} is a special case of the standard \emph{gauging} technique in Sturm--Liouville theory: \(H_g\) is obtained from \(-\partial_u^2\) by conjugation with the multiplication operator \(M_g : f \mapsto gf\). The identity is classical; the specific content of the paper lies in the choice \eqref{eq:g} and the numerical consequences of Sections~\ref{sec:breaking} and~\ref{sec:L}.

Corollary~\ref{cor:eigen} is a statement about formal eigenfunctions. It does not promote the algebraic relation to an equality of self-adjoint operators on a Hilbert space, nor does it guarantee coincidence of boundary conditions in any self-adjoint realization. \(H_g\) is not symmetric on \(C_c^\infty(0,2\pi)\) in the standard \(L^2\) inner product; a genuine spectral analysis requires choosing a domain invariant under \(H_g\) and analyzing its adjoint. This is not attempted here.
\end{remark}

% ============================================================
\section{Symmetry argument and its failure}
\label{sec:breaking}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Parity of the states and of the transition operator}

Consider the reflection \(\hat{P} : u \mapsto 2\pi - u\). Direct computation gives
\begin{align}
Y_n(2\pi - u) &= (-1)^{n+1} Y_n(u), \label{eq:parity-Y} \\
D(2\pi - u) &= -D(u) = (-1)^{1} D(u). \label{eq:parity-D}
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{equation}
(Y_n D Y_m)(2\pi - u) = (-1)^{n+m+3} (Y_n D Y_m)(u). \label{eq:parity-product}
\end{equation}
In particular:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For \(n = 1\), \(m = 3\): exponent \(7\) is odd, so \(Y_1 D Y_3\) is \textbf{odd} under \(\hat{P}\).
    \item For \(n = 1\), \(m = 2\): exponent \(6\) is even, so \(Y_1 D Y_2\) is \textbf{even} under \(\hat{P}\). Moreover, a direct computation gives
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Y1DY2}
    Y_1(u) D(u) Y_2(u) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^2(u),
    \end{equation}
    which is non-negative on \((0, 2\pi)\) and positive except on a set of measure zero.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Vanishing in the symmetric reference model}

\begin{proposition}[Vanishing in the reference model]
\label{prop:symmetry}
Let \(h \in C^0(0, 2\pi)\) satisfy \(h(2\pi - u) = h(u)\) and \(h > 0\) on \((0, 2\pi)\), with \eqref{eq:tilde-Nn}. Define
\[
\tilde{\Psi}_n(u) := \frac{h(u) Y_n(u)}{\sqrt{\tilde{N}_n}}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3,
\]
and \(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{mn} := \int_0^{2\pi} \tilde{\Psi}_m(u) D(u) \tilde{\Psi}_n(u) \, du\). Then
\[
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{13} = 0, \qquad \tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{12} > 0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(h\) is even under \(\hat{P}\), so is \(h^2\). The integrand of \(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{13}\) is proportional to \(h^2 (Y_1 D Y_3)\), an even function times an odd function, hence is odd. Its integral over \((0, 2\pi)\) vanishes.

For \(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{12}\), by \eqref{eq:Y1DY2} the integrand is
\[
\tilde{\Psi}_1(u) D(u) \tilde{\Psi}_2(u) = \frac{h(u)^2}{\sqrt{\tilde{N}_1 \tilde{N}_2}} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \sin^2(u).
\]
Since \(h > 0\) and \(\sin^2(u) > 0\) almost everywhere on \((0, 2\pi)\), the integrand is strictly positive almost everywhere. With \eqref{eq:tilde-Nn}, this yields \(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{12} > 0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The asymmetric weight and numerical symmetry-breaking}

The weight \(g\) of \eqref{eq:g} does \emph{not} satisfy \(g(2\pi - u) = g(u)\); the ratio \(g(2\pi - u)/g(u)\) differs from one. Consequently, Proposition~\ref{prop:symmetry} does not apply, and the vanishing of \(\mathcal{A}_{13}\) is not expected to persist.

\begin{observation}[Numerical symmetry-breaking]
\label{obs:numerical}
For \(x_p = 21\), \(D(u) = \cos(u/2)\), and the normalization \eqref{eq:Amn}, composite Simpson quadrature with \(N\) subintervals gives the values in Table~\ref{tab:conv}. The reference value \(R^{*} \approx 0.6035155247873\) is computed independently by adaptive quadrature (\texttt{scipy.integrate.quad}, \texttt{limit}$=300$); the corresponding reference matrix elements are \(\mathcal{A}_{12}^{*} \approx 0.6220748484804\) and \(\mathcal{A}_{13}^{*} \approx -0.4832666943981\).
\end{observation}

\section{The operator \(L\) and its formal zero mode}
\label{sec:L}
% ============================================================

\subsection{The operator \(L\)}

Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L}
L := -\partial_u^2 + V_{\text{eff}}(u), \qquad V_{\text{eff}}(u) := \frac{g''(u)}{g(u)}.
\end{equation}

The operators \(H_g\) of \eqref{eq:Hg} and \(L\) of \eqref{eq:L} are distinct differential operators constructed from the same function \(g\). The gauging identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauging} concerns \(H_g\); the formal zero-mode identity and factorization for \(L\) are established separately below. No claim of equivalence between the two operators is made here.

\subsection{The formal zero mode}

\begin{proposition}[Formal zero mode]
\label{prop:zero-mode}
For \(g \in C^2(0,2\pi)\) with \(g > 0\) on \((0, 2\pi)\) and \(V_{\text{eff}} := g''/g\), the function \(g\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Lg0}
L g = 0 \quad \text{on } (0, 2\pi).
\end{equation}
Moreover, with \(p := g'/g\), the operator \(L\) factors formally as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factorization}
L = (-\partial_u - p)(\partial_u - p).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity \eqref{eq:Lg0} is a direct computation: \(Lg = -g'' + (g''/g)g = 0\).

For \eqref{eq:factorization}, apply both sides to \(f \in C^2(0,2\pi)\):
\begin{align*}
(-\partial_u - p)(\partial_u - p) f &= -\partial_u(\partial_u f - pf) - p(\partial_u f - pf) \\
&= -\partial_u^2 f + (p' + p^2) f.
\end{align*}
Since \(p' + p^2 = g''/g = V_{\text{eff}}\), the right-hand side equals \(Lf\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:zero-mode} is an \emph{algebraic} statement on the open interval. It does not assert that \(g\) is a norm-square-integrable eigenfunction of a self-adjoint realization of \(L\) on \(L^2((0,2\pi))\). The potential \(V_{\text{eff}}\) has a strong singularity at \(u = 2\pi\) (Section~\ref{sec:asymptotics}); a careful analysis of the self-adjoint extensions of \(L\) is required before any spectral statement can be made, and is not attempted here.
\end{remark}

\section{Asymptotics of \(g\) and \(V_{\text{eff}}\)}
\label{sec:asymptotics}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Near \(u = 0^{+}\).} As \(u \to 0^{+}\),
\[
g(u; x_p) = O\!\left(u^{5} \ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) e^{-2\pi/(x_p u)}\right).
\]
Since the exponential dominates any power of \(u\) and any power of \(\ln(2\pi/u)\), we have \(g(u) = O(u^N)\) as \(u \to 0^{+}\) for every \(N \in \mathbb{N}\), and \(g \to 0\).

The potential \(V_{\text{eff}}\) is dominated by the \(2\pi/(x_p u^2)\) term in \(p\), so \(p(u) \sim 2\pi/(x_p u^2)\) and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Veff-u0}
V_{\text{eff}}(u) = p(u)^2 + p'(u) \sim \frac{4\pi^2}{x_p^2 u^4} \to +\infty \quad (u \to 0^{+}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Near \(u = 2\pi^{-}\).} Set \(t := 2\pi - u > 0\). Since \(\ln(2\pi/u) \sim t/(2\pi)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:g-u2pi}
g(u) = \frac{e^{-1/21}}{2\pi} \cdot t \cdot \left(1 + O(t)\right) = C\, t + O(t^2), \qquad C = \frac{e^{-1/21}}{2\pi} \text{ for } x_p = 21.
\end{equation}
Thus \(g\) vanishes linearly as \(u \to 2\pi^{-}\). Correspondingly,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Veff-u2pi}
V_{\text{eff}}(u) \sim -\frac{191}{42\pi(2\pi - u)} \to -\infty \quad (u \to 2\pi^{-}).
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}
The two endpoint behaviors have different singular orders: a positive fourth-order pole at \(u = 0\), and a negative first-order pole at \(u = 2\pi\). The potential is not of the standard form required for a regular Sturm--Liouville problem; both endpoints are singular. Correspondingly, \(g\) vanishes super-polynomially at \(u = 0\) but only linearly at \(u = 2\pi\).
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion and open questions}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Summary}

The paper establishes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The gauging identity \(H_g(gY) = -g\,Y''\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:gauging}).
    \item The vanishing of \(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{13}\) in a symmetric reference model (Proposition~\ref{prop:symmetry}), made precise by the explicit identity \eqref{eq:Y1DY2}.
    \item For the specified setup, the reproducible numerical value \(R_{13/12} \approx 0.6035155248\) at \(x_p = 21\) (Observation~\ref{obs:numerical}).
    \item The formal zero-mode identity \(Lg = 0\) and factorization \(L = (-\partial_u - p)(\partial_u - p)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:zero-mode}).
\end{enumerate}

The scope of these claims, and the list of what is not established, is given in Section~\ref{sec:scope}.

\subsection{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Domain and boundary conditions for \(L\).} Determine the self-adjoint extensions of \(L\) on \(L^2((0,2\pi))\), and whether any admits \(g\) as a genuine eigenstate. The singularity structure \eqref{eq:Veff-u0}--\eqref{eq:Veff-u2pi} suggests that limit-circle behavior may be relevant at \(u = 2\pi\).
    
    \item \emph{Spectral density of \(L\).} If \(L\) admits a self-adjoint realization with discrete spectrum \(\{\lambda_n\}\), determine the asymptotic behavior of \(\lambda_n\) as \(n \to \infty\) and the properties of the associated spectral zeta function.
    
    \item \emph{Dependence of \(R_{13/12}\) on \(x_p\).} A systematic study of \(R_{13/12}(x_p)\) across \((0, \infty)\) is left for future work; no claim about monotonicity or continuity in \(x_p\) is made here.
    
    \item \emph{Alternative normalizations.} The choice \eqref{eq:Amn} reflects a specific weighted \(L^2\) inner product. How does \(R_{13/12}\) vary under alternative normalizations, and what is the correct interpretation?
\end{enumerate}

from scipy.integrate import quad

def g(u, xp=21.0):
    if u <= 0 or u >= 2*np.pi:
        return 0.0
    return (u/(2*np.pi))**5 * np.log(2*np.pi/u) * np.exp(-2*np.pi/(xp*u))

def Y(n, u):
    return np.sin(n*u/2.0)

def D(u):
    return np.cos(u/2.0)

def simpson(f, a, b, N):
    if N % 2 != 0:
        N += 1
    h = (b - a) / N
    s = f(a) + f(b)
    for i in range(1, N, 2):
        s += 4 * f(a + i*h)
    for i in range(2, N, 2):
        s += 2 * f(a + i*h)
    return s * h / 3

def ratio_simpson(N, xp=21.0):
    a, b = 0.0, 2*np.pi
    N1 = simpson(lambda u: (g(u,xp)*Y(1,u))**2, a, b, N)
    N2 = simpson(lambda u: (g(u,xp)*Y(2,u))**2, a, b, N)
    N3 = simpson(lambda u: (g(u,xp)*Y(3,u))**2, a, b, N)
    A12 = simpson(lambda u:
        (g(u,xp)*Y(1,u)/np.sqrt(N1)) * D(u) * (g(u,xp)*Y(2,u)/np.sqrt(N2)),
        a, b, N)
    A13 = simpson(lambda u:
        (g(u,xp)*Y(1,u)/np.sqrt(N1)) * D(u) * (g(u,xp)*Y(3,u)/np.sqrt(N3)),
        a, b, N)
    return A12, A13, (A13/A12)**2

def reference_value(xp=21.0, limit=300):
    N1 = quad(lambda u: (g(u,xp)*Y(1,u))**2, 0, 2*np.pi, limit=limit)[0]
    N2 = quad(lambda u: (g(u,xp)*Y(2,u))**2, 0, 2*np.pi, limit=limit)[0]
    N3 = quad(lambda u: (g(u,xp)*Y(3,u))**2, 0, 2*np.pi, limit=limit)[0]
    A12 = quad(lambda u:
        (g(u,xp)*Y(1,u)/np.sqrt(N1)) * D(u) * (g(u,xp)*Y(2,u)/np.sqrt(N2)),
        0, 2*np.pi, limit=limit)[0]
    A13 = quad(lambda u:
        (g(u,xp)*Y(1,u)/np.sqrt(N1)) * D(u) * (g(u,xp)*Y(3,u)/np.sqrt(N3)),
        0, 2*np.pi, limit=limit)[0]
    return A12, A13, (A13/A12)**2

if __name__ == '__main__':
    xp = 21.0
    A12r, A13r, Rr = reference_value(xp)
    print(f"Reference (adaptive quad):")
    print(f"  A12 = {A12r:.13f}")
    print(f"  A13 = {A13r:.13f}")
    print(f"  R   = {Rr:.13f}")
    print()
    print(f"Composite Simpson convergence at xp = {xp}:")
    print(f"{'N':>6} | {'R':>16} | {'|R-R*|':>12} | {'|R-0.60352|':>12}")
    print("-" * 60)
    for N in [20, 40, 80, 200, 400]:
        A12, A13, R = ratio_simpson(N, xp)
        print(f"{N:>6} | {R:.13f} | {abs(R-Rr):.4e}   | {abs(R-0.60352):.4e}")
\end{verbatim}

Values in the last one or two digits may vary across platforms due to
floating-point accumulation in the adaptive quadrature routine and in the
composite Simpson sums. The values above are for reference and correspond to
\texttt{numpy}~1.26 and \texttt{scipy}~1.13 on a standard x86-64 platform.

The rounded value \(0.60352\) appearing in the last column of
Table~\ref{tab:conv} is a rounding, not an exact value; the difference
\(|R^{*} - 0.60352| \approx 4.47 \times 10^{-6}\) is therefore a floor,
not a measure of quadrature error.
