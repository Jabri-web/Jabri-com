Opening — Problem, Achievement, Type of Evidence

Problem

Which differential operator can be constructed from the envelope \(g(u)\) shared by Frames 1 and 3? Is this operator derived from the master equation of Frame 1, or is it a compatible choice? What structural properties (formal zero mode, endpoint classification) can be established, and what constraints do they impose on the spectral problem?

Achievement

Construction of \(L=-\partial_u^2+g''/g\), proof of the structural identity \(Lg=0\), the formal factorization, endpoint singularity analysis, Limit Point/Circle classification, and a conditional definition of the spectral zeta function. Additionally: a precise algebraic analysis (§2) shows that the exact transformation of the master equation yields a different normalized coefficient \(V_{\mathrm{new}} = (4\pi^2/u^4)Q\); and a local argument at \(u=\pi\) rejects the standard first-order Darboux transformation.

Type of Evidence

Exact differential identities on the open interval. Frobenius analysis for endpoint classification. Explicit algebraic derivation (§2) showing the non-equivalence of \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\), with a local proof rejecting the standard first-order Darboux path through \(\pi\). Generalized Darboux transformations are outside the scope of the proof. Choice of self-adjoint realization, lower bound, eigenvalues, and zeta convergence remain open and are documented as next steps, not claimed results.

1. From Frame 3's Operator to a Spectral Problem

Frame 3 established the algebraic identity:

\[ H_g(gY) = g(-Y'') \]

On this basis, one may define a Schrödinger-type operator:

\[ L = -\frac{d^2}{du^2} + V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u), \qquad V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u) = \frac{g''(u)}{g(u)} \]

Formal Zero Mode and Supersymmetric Factorization

Structural property of the operator. From the definition \(V_{\mathrm{eff}} = g''/g\): \[ Lg = -g'' + \frac{g''}{g}g = 0 \] so the envelope \(g(u)\) satisfies the operator equation on the open interval and is a formal zero-mode. Whether \(g\) is an actual eigenfunction with eigenvalue zero for a self-adjoint realization of \(L\) requires specifying the domain and verifying the boundary conditions.

The operator also admits the algebraic factorization \[ L = \left(-\frac{d}{du} - p\right)\left(\frac{d}{du} - p\right), \qquad p = \frac{g'}{g} \] This is a formal differential identity on smooth functions on the interval. Since \(p=g'/g\), we have \((\partial_u - p)g=0\); however, this does not decide whether \(g\) belongs to the kernel of a specific self-adjoint realization, which depends on the domain and boundary conditions.

Asymptotic behaviour of \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)\)

Using \(V_{\mathrm{eff}} = p' + p^2\) with \(p(u) = \frac{5}{u} - \frac{1}{u\ln(2\pi/u)} + \frac{2\pi}{21u^2}\):

  • As \(u\to 0^+\): the \(2\pi/(21u^2)\) term dominates, giving \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)\sim \dfrac{4\pi^2}{441u^4}\to+\infty\). Order-4 singularity.
  • As \(u\to 2\pi^-\): we find \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)\sim -\dfrac{191}{42\pi(2\pi-u)}\to-\infty\). Order-1 singularity.
Derivation of the asymptotic limit at \(u \to 2\pi^-\)

Set \(t = 2\pi - u\) and \(\delta = t/(2\pi)\), so \(x = u/(2\pi) = 1-\delta\). Expand each factor:

\[ x^5 = 1 - 5\delta + 10\delta^2 + O(\delta^3), \] \[ \ln(1/x) = \delta + \tfrac12\delta^2 + O(\delta^3), \] \[ e^{-1/(21x)} = e^{-1/21}\left(1 - \tfrac{\delta}{21} - \tfrac{41}{882}\delta^2 + O(\delta^3)\right). \]

Multiplying: \(g(u) = e^{-1/21}\left[\delta - \tfrac{191}{42}\delta^2 + O(\delta^3)\right]\), i.e. \(g(u) \approx C\,t + a\,t^2\) with \(C = e^{-1/21}/(2\pi)\) and \(a = -191\,e^{-1/21}/(168\pi^2)\). Hence

\[ V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u) = \frac{g''(u)}{g(u)} \sim \frac{2a}{C\,t} = -\frac{191}{42\pi\,(2\pi-u)}. \]

2. Verification of Origin — Is Frame 4's Operator Derived from the Master Equation?

Question of this section. Frame 4's operator is \(L = -\partial_u^2 + g''/g\). Can it be extracted from the master equation of Frame 1 — after the change of variable \(x\to u\) and elimination of the first derivative — or is it a compatible choice that retains the envelope \(g\) and discards the oscillatory factor \(\sin u\)?

2.1 Change of variable: \(x = 2\pi/u\)

\[ \frac{du}{dx} = -\frac{u^2}{2\pi}, \quad \partial_x = -\frac{u^2}{2\pi}\,\partial_u, \quad \partial_x^2 = \frac{u^4}{4\pi^2}\,\partial_u^2 + \frac{u^3}{2\pi^2}\,\partial_u \]

The first-order term arises from differentiating the coefficient, and cannot be dropped.

2.2 The master operator in \(u\)

\[ H_u = -\frac{u^4}{4\pi^2}\,\partial_u^2 - \frac{u^3}{2\pi^2}\,\partial_u + Q\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) \]

Abbreviate \(A(u) = u^4/(4\pi^2) > 0\) and \(B(u) = u^3/(2\pi^2) > 0\), so \(H_u = -A\partial_u^2 - B\partial_u + Q\).

2.3 The key identity

Substituting \(x = 2\pi/u\) in the master function:

\[ y(u) := Z\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) = g(u)\sin(u), \qquad g(u) = \left(\frac{u}{2\pi}\right)^5 \ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) e^{-2\pi/(21u)} \]

An exact identity that separates the envelope \(g(u)\) from the oscillatory factor \(\sin u\).

2.4 Elimination of the first derivative

Set \(y = w/u\) (i.e. \(w = uy\)). Then

\[ y' = \frac{w'}{u} - \frac{w}{u^2}, \qquad y'' = \frac{w''}{u} - \frac{2w'}{u^2} + \frac{2w}{u^3} \]

Substituting into \(H_u y = 0\) and multiplying by \(u\):

\[ -A\,w'' + \left(\frac{2A}{u} - B\right) w' + \left(-\frac{2A}{u^2} + \frac{B}{u} + Q\right) w = 0 \]

The first-derivative coefficient vanishes identically from the definitions of \(A\) and \(B\):

\[ \frac{2A}{u} - B = \frac{u^3}{2\pi^2} - \frac{u^3}{2\pi^2} = 0, \qquad -\frac{2A}{u^2} + \frac{B}{u} = -\frac{u^2}{2\pi^2} + \frac{u^2}{2\pi^2} = 0 \]

No assumption is required; the cancellation is automatic. The remaining equation is

\[ \boxed{\,A\,w'' = Q\,w\,} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad w'' = \frac{4\pi^2}{u^4}\,Q\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right) w \]

2.5 The normalized coefficient

In the Schrödinger convention \(-w'' + V_{\mathrm{new}} w = 0\):

\[ \boxed{\,V_{\mathrm{new}}(u) = \frac{4\pi^2}{u^4}\,Q\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{u}\right)\,} \]

The factor \(4\pi^2/u^4\) is a direct artefact of the change of variable. Note that \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) is defined on \((0,\pi)\cup(\pi,2\pi)\) only, because \(Q(2)\) is undefined (zero of \(Z\) at \(x=2\)).

Caveat on spectral equivalence. \(V_{\mathrm{new}}(u) := (4\pi^2/u^4)Q(2\pi/u)\) is the coefficient in the transformed equation \(-w'' + V_{\mathrm{new}}w = 0\), obtained after the change of variable \(x = 2\pi/u\) and the substitution \(w = uy\). This is an identity between differential expressions on \((0,2\pi)\). Spectral equivalence between two self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces additionally requires matching the measure, the domain, and the boundary conditions. Establishing such equivalence (or proving its impossibility) is a separate question, and is not claimed here.

2.6 Darboux-equivalence test

Explicit difference:

\[ \Delta V := V_{\mathrm{new}} - V_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2\,\frac{g'}{g}\,\cot u - 1 + \frac{2}{u}\frac{g'}{g} + \frac{2}{u}\cot u \]

Standard first-order Darboux condition. Equivalence of \(L_1 = -\partial^2 + V_1\) and \(L_2 = -\partial^2 + V_2\) requires a seed \(\varphi\) satisfying

\[ -\varphi'' + V_1\varphi = \lambda\varphi \quad (\text{for some } \lambda), \qquad V_2 = V_1 - 2(\ln\varphi)'' \]

Local argument at \(u=\pi\). We show that no standard first-order Darboux transformation (at any \(\lambda\)) produces \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) from \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\).

Facts:

  • \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is smooth at \(u=\pi\) (since \(g(\pi)\neq 0\)).
  • \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) has a non-zero simple pole at \(u=\pi\) with residue \(C_\pi := \dfrac{2(128\ln 2 - 21)}{21\pi\ln 2} \approx 2.96\) (see §2.7).

Since \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is smooth, every non-zero solution \(\varphi\) of \(-\varphi'' + V_{\mathrm{eff}}\varphi = \lambda\varphi\) is smooth in a neighbourhood of \(u=\pi\) (standard ODE theory). Hence:

  • If \(\varphi(\pi)\neq 0\): \((\ln\varphi)''\) is smooth at \(\pi\), so \(V_D = V_{\mathrm{eff}} - 2(\ln\varphi)''\) is smooth at \(\pi\) — no pole.
  • If \(\varphi(\pi)=0\): then \(\varphi'(\pi)\neq 0\) (otherwise \(\varphi\equiv 0\) by uniqueness). So \(\varphi(u)\sim C_0(u-\pi)\), giving \[ (\ln\varphi)'' \sim -\frac{1}{(u-\pi)^2}, \qquad V_D \sim \frac{2}{(u-\pi)^2} \] i.e. a double pole with residue \(2\) — not a simple pole, and not with residue \(C_\pi\approx 2.96\).

In neither case can \(V_D\) equal \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) (which has a simple pole with residue \(C_\pi\approx 2.96\) at \(\pi\)). This is a complete local proof: no seed \(\varphi\) satisfying the standard first-order Darboux conditions can produce \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\).

Verdict (upgraded from "open" to "rejected for the standard class"). No standard first-order Darboux transformation (with any seed \(\varphi\) solving \(-\varphi''+V_{\mathrm{eff}}\varphi=\lambda\varphi\)) relates \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) to \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\). The proof is local at \(u=\pi\) and uses only:
  • smoothness of \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) at \(\pi\);
  • existence of a non-zero simple pole in \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) at \(\pi\);
  • the local behaviour of the seed at its zero (simple, not double).
Scope. Standard first-order Darboux transformations only. Generalized transformations (higher-order, Darboux–Goncharenko, or seeds not constrained to solve the eigenvalue equation) fall outside this argument and have not been investigated here.

2.7 Comparison of singular structure

To pin down the residue \(C_\pi\) used in the argument, we derive the local expansion. Write the master function explicitly:

\[ Z(x) = x^{-5} \ln(x)\, e^{-x/21} \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{x}\right), \qquad x>1 \]

The table below records the behaviour of both potentials:

Endpoint \(V_{\mathrm{eff}} = g''/g\) \(V_{\mathrm{new}} = (4\pi^2/u^4)Q\) Observation
\(u\to 0^+\) \(\sim \dfrac{4\pi^2}{441u^4}\to+\infty\) \(\sim \dfrac{4\pi^2}{441u^4}\to+\infty\) Same leading behaviour (order-4 pole)
\(u=\pi\) Smooth, finite (\(g(\pi)\neq 0\)) Simple pole \(\sim \dfrac{C_\pi}{u-\pi}\), \(C_\pi\approx 2.96\) Structural difference: \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) has an interior simple pole, \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) does not
\(u\to 2\pi^-\) \(\sim -\dfrac{191}{42\pi t}\to-\infty\) \(\sim +\dfrac{2}{t^2}\to+\infty\) \(t=2\pi-u\). Simple negative vs. double positive

Reading the table. \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\):

  • agree as \(u\to 0^+\) (same order-4 pole);
  • differ fundamentally at \(u=\pi\): \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) smooth, \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) has a simple pole;
  • differ fundamentally as \(u\to 2\pi^-\): simple negative vs. double positive.

This structural mismatch is what makes the local argument in §2.6 possible, and confirms the rejection of the standard first-order Darboux equivalence.

Details: singularity of \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) as \(u\to 2\pi^-\)

Set \(t = 2\pi-u \to 0^+\), so \(x = 2\pi/u = 2\pi/(2\pi-t) = 1/(1 - t/(2\pi))\). Hence

\[ x - 1 = \frac{t}{2\pi - t} \sim \frac{t}{2\pi} > 0. \]

Expanding \(Z(x)\) near \(x=1\):

\[ Z(x) \sim -2\pi e^{-1/21}\,(x-1)^2 \quad \text{(double zero)} \] \[ Z''(x) \sim -4\pi e^{-1/21}, \qquad Q(x) = \frac{Z''(x)}{Z(x)} \sim \frac{2}{(x-1)^2} \]

Since \(x-1 \sim t/(2\pi)\), we get \(Q \sim 2/(t/(2\pi))^2 = 8\pi^2/t^2\), and therefore

\[ V_{\mathrm{new}} = \frac{4\pi^2}{u^4}\,Q \sim \frac{4\pi^2}{16\pi^4}\cdot\frac{8\pi^2}{t^2} = \frac{2}{t^2}. \]
Details: residue \(C_\pi\) at \(u=\pi\)

Set \(x=2+\varepsilon\); the zero of \(Z\) at \(x=2\) is simple. Expand the four factors of \(Z\):

\[ (2+\varepsilon)^{-5} = 2^{-5}\left(1 + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-5} = 2^{-5}\left(1 - \tfrac{5}{2}\varepsilon + O(\varepsilon^2)\right), \] \[ \ln(2+\varepsilon) = \ln 2 \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\varepsilon}{2\ln 2} + O(\varepsilon^2)\right), \] \[ e^{-(2+\varepsilon)/21} = e^{-2/21}\left(1 - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{21} + O(\varepsilon^2)\right), \] \[ \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{2+\varepsilon}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2}\varepsilon - \frac{\pi}{4}\varepsilon^2 + O(\varepsilon^3). \]

Writing \(Z(2+\varepsilon) = \alpha\varepsilon + \beta\varepsilon^2 + O(\varepsilon^3)\), we obtain

\[ \frac{\beta}{\alpha} = \frac{21 - 128\ln 2}{42\ln 2} \approx -2.32627. \]

Since \(Q(x) \sim 2(\beta/\alpha)/(x-2)\) near \(x=2\), and using \(x = 2\pi/u\), at \(x=2\) we have \(u=\pi\) with \(\delta x = -2\pi/u^2\,\delta u = -2\delta u/\pi\). Thus \(\varepsilon \approx -\tfrac{2}{\pi}(u-\pi)\), and

\[ V_{\mathrm{new}} \sim -\frac{4(\beta/\alpha)}{\pi(u-\pi)} = \underbrace{\frac{2(128\ln 2 - 21)}{21\pi\ln 2}}_{\approx\,2.9619}\cdot\frac{1}{u-\pi}. \]

This establishes a simple pole with non-zero residue \(C_\pi\approx 2.96\), the foundation of the argument in §2.6.

Conclusion

Frame 4's operator, \(L = -\partial_u^2 + g''/g\), is not derived algebraically from the master equation of Frame 1. The exact transformation yields \(V_{\mathrm{new}} = (4\pi^2/u^4)Q\), differing from \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\):

  • as \(u\to 0^+\): leading behaviour matches;
  • at \(u=\pi\): \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) smooth, \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) has a simple pole of residue \(\approx 2.96\);
  • as \(u\to 2\pi^-\): \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) simple negative pole, \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) double positive pole.

The standard first-order Darboux transformation is rejected by the local argument at \(u=\pi\). Generalized transformations remain outside the scope of this argument. Frame 4 is a legitimate spectral problem, but it is a compatible choice, not a derived consequence.

Open question worth pursuing

Does a generalized Darboux transformation (higher order, Darboux–Goncharenko, or a seed not constrained to solve the eigenvalue equation) relate \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) to \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\)? This is an independent question, outside the scope of §2, but it is the precise form of the remaining question once the standard class is closed.

3. The Envelope as a Natural Regulator (Research Hypothesis)

In quantum field theory, the vacuum energy computation requires summing zero-point energies. One working hypothesis is that the envelope \(g(u)\) acts as a natural regulator in \(L\).

This remains a hypothesis until one establishes that the high-energy spectral behaviour of \(L\) yields convergence. The next step is to derive the high-energy asymptotics of the eigenvalues \(\lambda_n\), and thereby decide whether the zero-point sum converges or requires zeta regularization.

4. Spectral Zeta Function and Vacuum Energy

If the non-zero spectrum is positive and the zero-mode treatment is specified, the spectral zeta function on the non-zero spectrum is defined by

\[ \zeta_{L,\ne0}(s) = \sum_{\lambda_n>0} \lambda_n^{-s} \]

Provided the appropriate analytic continuation exists, the zeta-regularized vacuum energy is defined by

\[ E_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{reg}} = \frac{1}{2}\zeta_{L,\ne0}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) \]

If the \(\lambda_n\) are squares of physical frequencies, the above corresponds to regularizing \(\sum_{\lambda_n>0}\sqrt{\lambda_n}\) with the zero mode removed, in units \(\hbar=1\).

Conditional definition, not a computed result. The expression \(E_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{reg}} = \tfrac12\zeta_{L,\ne 0}(-1/2)\) is a proposed definition, not a value. Its numerical evaluation requires:
  • establishing that the zeta series converges in some range;
  • establishing the appropriate analytic continuation to \(s=-1/2\);
  • handling any scale dependence or regularization subtraction imposed by the physical setup.
Treatment of the zero mode. The function \(g(u)\) is square-integrable on \((0,2\pi)\). Near \(0^+\), it decays faster than any power because of the factor \(e^{-2\pi/(21u)}\). Near \(2\pi^-\), \(g(u) \sim \frac{e^{-1/21}}{2\pi}(2\pi-u)\), also integrable. Hence \(g \in L^2((0,2\pi))\).

However, \(L^2\) membership alone does not establish that \(g\) lies in the domain of a self-adjoint realization of \(L\); the latter requires specifying a self-adjoint extension and verifying the domain conditions.

Next analytic step: local behaviour of solutions near the endpoints

Choosing a self-adjoint realization requires more than checking normalizability of \(g\); it requires studying the solutions of \(L\psi=\lambda\psi\) near both endpoints. §5 performs this step.

Computational work plan

  1. Analyze the solutions near each endpoint and identify their asymptotic behaviour.
  2. Specify domain, boundary conditions, and a self-adjoint realization.
  3. Test whether the operator is bounded below.
  4. Compute the eigenvalues numerically.
  5. Study the convergence of the spectral zeta and its continuation to \(s=-1/2\).

§5 carries out steps 1 and 2.

5. Appendix: Local Behaviour of Solutions and Boundary Conditions

This appendix carries out step 1 of the work plan: analyzing the local behaviour of solutions near both endpoints and classifying the endpoints.

A. Endpoint \(u \to 2\pi^-\): Limit Circle

Set \(t = 2\pi - u \to 0^+\). The potential:

\[ V_{\mathrm{eff}}(t) \sim -\frac{c}{t}, \qquad c = \frac{191}{42\pi} > 0. \]

The eigenvalue equation near \(t=0\), neglecting \(\lambda\psi\) at leading order:

\[ \psi''(t) + \frac{c}{t}\psi(t) \approx 0. \]

Frobenius analysis. Write \(\psi = t^r\sum_{n\ge 0}a_n t^n\) with \(a_0 \ne 0\). The indicial equation at order \(t^0\) gives \(r(r-1) = 0\), hence \(r\in\{0,1\}\).

Branch \(r=1\): the recursion at the next order gives \(a_1 = -c\,a_0/2\), so

\[ \psi_1(t) = a_0\left(t - \frac{c}{2}t^2 + O(t^3)\right). \]

Branch \(r=0\) is resonant; the second solution requires logarithmic terms:

\[ \psi_2(t) = 1 - c\,t\ln t + O(t^2\ln t) \quad \text{(with a suitable normalization)}. \]

Square-integrability. \(\int_0^\varepsilon |\psi_1|^2 dt \sim \int_0^\varepsilon t^2 dt < \infty\), and \(\int_0^\varepsilon |\psi_2|^2 dt \sim \int_0^\varepsilon (1 - ct\ln t)^2 dt < \infty\) (since \(t\ln t \to 0\)). Both solutions belong to \(L^2\); the endpoint is Limit Circle.

Boundary conditions at a Limit Circle endpoint. At a Limit Circle endpoint, the space of locally square-integrable solutions is two-dimensional, so \(L^2\) alone does not select a unique solution. A self-adjoint realization is specified by a boundary condition on the Wronskian boundary trace.

Concretely, the maximal domain \(\mathcal{D}_{\max}\) consists of functions \(\psi\) with \(\psi, \psi'\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}\) and \(L\psi\in L^2\) locally; on this domain the Wronskian boundary form \[ [\psi, \chi]_0 := \lim_{t\to 0}\left[\psi(t)\overline{\chi'(t)} - \psi'(t)\overline{\chi(t)}\right] \] exists for all \(\psi, \chi\in\mathcal{D}_{\max}\) (a standard fact in the Limit Circle theory). The self-adjoint extensions are parameterized by a unitary \(U(1)\) family; the corresponding boundary conditions are expressed via this Lagrange/Wronskian boundary form, not via a direct Robin form \[ \alpha\,\psi(2\pi^-) + \beta\,\psi'(2\pi^-) = 0, \] because \(\psi_2'(t) \sim -c\ln t\) diverges logarithmically as \(t\to 0^+\), so the finite limit \(\psi'(2\pi^-)\) does not exist in general.

Dirichlet as a specific choice. The condition \(\psi(2\pi^-)=0\) is one particular member of the \(U(1)\) family, not the unique consequence of the classification. It is the natural candidate for a first study, since \(g\) satisfies it.
Result at \(2\pi^-\): Limit Circle. A self-adjoint boundary condition is required, expressed via the Lagrange/Wronskian boundary trace. Dirichlet is one specific choice among the \(U(1)\) family.

B. Endpoint \(u \to 0^+\): Limit Point

\[ V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u) \sim +\frac{C}{u^4}, \qquad C = \left(\frac{2\pi}{21}\right)^2 > 0. \]

Two independent solutions behave approximately as

\[ \psi_\pm(u) \sim \exp\!\left(\mp\frac{2\pi}{21u}\right). \]

\(\psi_+ \in L^2\) while \(\psi_- \notin L^2\). The endpoint is Limit Point: no extra boundary condition is needed; \(L^2\) automatically selects the admissible behaviour.

Result at \(0^+\): Limit Point. \(L^2\) selects the admissible behaviour automatically.

C. Combined result

  • At \(u=0^+\): Limit Point (no extra boundary condition).
  • At \(u=2\pi^-\): Limit Circle (boundary condition required; expressed via the Lagrange/Wronskian boundary trace, not via a direct Robin form).
  • The envelope \(g\) behaves at \(2\pi^-\) like \(\psi_1\), and satisfies \(g(2\pi^-)=0\). It therefore satisfies the Dirichlet condition, which remains one specific choice among the self-adjoint extensions, not the unique outcome of the classification.
  • Open question: does a specific realization (e.g. Dirichlet) make the operator bounded below? Is zero the ground-state energy?

6. Results and Scope

What this frame has established

  • Construction of an operator from the envelope: definition of \(L = -\partial_u^2 + g''/g\), and proof of the structural identity \(Lg=0\) on the open interval.
  • Algebraic analysis of origin (§2): the exact transformation of Frame 1 yields \(V_{\mathrm{new}} = (4\pi^2/u^4)Q\), not \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\). The vanishing of the first-derivative term is automatic. A local argument at \(u=\pi\) rejects the standard first-order Darboux transformation.
  • SUSY QM factorization: \(L = (-\partial_u - p)(\partial_u - p)\) as a formal identity.
  • Endpoint singularity analysis: \(1/u^4\) at \(0^+\), \(-1/(2\pi-u)\) at \(2\pi^-\), and a simple pole in \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) at \(u=\pi\) with no counterpart in \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\).
  • Endpoint classification: Limit Point at \(0^+\), Limit Circle at \(2\pi^-\). The boundary condition at \(2\pi^-\) is expressed via the Lagrange/Wronskian boundary trace; Dirichlet is one specific choice.
  • Definition of the spectral zeta function as a conditional definition.
  • Separation of what is established from what is open: distinction between the formal zero mode and the actual eigenfunction; between the envelope as a candidate regulator and a proven one; between Frame 4 as an independent operator and Frame 4 as a derived consequence (the latter rejected).

What comes next

  1. Choose a self-adjoint realization at \(2\pi^-\), starting with the Dirichlet condition \(\psi(2\pi^-)=0\) as the natural first candidate.
  2. Verify that \(g\) belongs to the domain of this realization.
  3. Prove existence of a lower bound on the spectrum.
  4. Compute the positive eigenvalues \(\lambda_n\) numerically.
  5. Only then: study the convergence of the zeta function and the possibility of defining a physical vacuum energy.

7. Supplementary Notes (behind the toggle)

This section is not essential for reading the main text; it collects additional derivations and interpretive notes. The reader interested in results alone will find them in §1–§6.

This section provides a detailed reading of what has been achieved and what has been left open.

1. The identity \(Lg = 0\): structurally guaranteed

By definition \(V_{\mathrm{eff}} = g''/g\), the identity \(Lg=0\) follows immediately. This is a precise mathematical result, not a numerical approximation. It does not, however, by itself establish that \(g\) is an actual eigenfunction; that requires specifying the domain and boundary conditions, as §5 does.

2. Consequence of §2: Frame 4 is a compatible choice, not a derivation

The algebraic analysis in §2 shows that the exact transformation of Frame 1 yields \(V_{\mathrm{new}} = (4\pi^2/u^4)Q\), which differs from \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\). The local argument at \(u=\pi\) rejects the standard first-order Darboux transformation (since \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is smooth at \(\pi\), \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\) has a simple pole of residue \(\approx 2.96\), and seed behaviour at zeros yields smooth or double-pole — never a simple pole). Generalized Darboux transformations are outside the scope of that argument. This is consistent with the methodological stance of the consolidated manuscript: the four frames are parallel paths, not a serial chain.

3. Endpoint classification

§5 derives Limit Point at \(0^+\), Limit Circle at \(2\pi^-\). This is a structural classification; it does not establish a lower bound for the spectrum. At the Limit Circle endpoint, the boundary condition is expressed via the Lagrange or reference Wronskian form, not by a direct Robin form (since \(\psi_2'\) diverges logarithmically).

4. Zeta regularization

Defining \(\zeta_{L,\ne 0}(s)\) and \(E_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is conditional on three issues: (a) knowing the series in some range, (b) the appropriate analytic continuation, (c) handling of scale dependence.

5. The envelope as a natural regulator

A hypothesis, not a result. Proving it requires establishing the high-energy behaviour of the eigenvalues.

6. Note on \(R_{13/12}\)

A numerical result from Frame 3. It does not establish the spectral properties of Frame 4, nor does it predict the vacuum energy. The link between the frames is the shared envelope, not a derivation of the operator.

Summary

This frame produced: the operator, the structural identity, the formal factorization, the endpoint analysis, the endpoint classification, and an algebraic demonstration that the operator is a compatible choice, not a derived consequence, together with a local proof rejecting the standard first-order Darboux transformation. It leaves open: generalized Darboux transformations, self-adjoint realization, lower bound, eigenvalue computation, and zeta convergence.
