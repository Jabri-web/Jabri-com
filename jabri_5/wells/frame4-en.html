Frame 4: Towards a Spectral Problem

Towards a Spectral Problem, Zero Mode, and Zeta Regularization
Eng. Abdulla M. N. Al-Jabri — Sana'a, Yemen | October 2026

1. From the Frame 3 Operator to a Spectral Problem

Frame 3 established the algebraic identity:

\[ H_g(gY) = g(-Y'') \]

Accordingly, an independent Schrödinger operator can be defined as:

\[ L = -\frac{d^2}{du^2} + V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u), \qquad V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u) = \frac{g''(u)}{g(u)} \]

Zero Mode and Supersymmetric Factorization

Structural Property of the Operator: From the potential definition \(V_{\mathrm{eff}} = g''/g\), we obtain the fundamental identity: \[ Lg = -g'' + \frac{g''}{g}g = 0 \] This means the envelope \(g(u)\) is a Zero-Mode solution of \(L\) on the interior domain. Furthermore, the operator can be algebraically factorized as: \[ L = \left(-\frac{d}{du} - p\right)\left(\frac{d}{du} - p\right), \qquad p = \frac{g'}{g} \] This factorization aligns with the mathematical structure of Supersymmetric Quantum Mechanics (SUSY QM). However, the formal factorization alone does not prove that every self-adjoint realization of the operator is non-negative; this depends on the domain and boundary conditions. Likewise, calling it the "ground state" requires proving it is an allowed, normalizable eigenstate, and that zero is the lowest eigenvalue.

Asymptotic Behavior of \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)\)

Using \(V_{\mathrm{eff}} = p' + p^2\), where \(p(u) = \frac{5}{u} - \frac{1}{u\ln(2\pi/u)} + \frac{2\pi}{21u^2}\), the endpoint behavior is:

  • As \(u\to0^+\): The term \(2\pi/(21u^2)\) dominates, yielding \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)\sim \frac{4\pi^2}{441u^4}\to+\infty\).
  • As \(u\to2\pi^-\): Letting \(t=2\pi-u\), the envelope behaves as \(g(u) \approx C t\), yielding \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)\sim -\frac{191}{42\pi(2\pi-u)}\).

This constrains the analysis of singular endpoints and helps determine appropriate boundary conditions, but does not uniquely determine them on its own.

2. The Envelope as a Candidate Natural Regulator (Research Hypothesis)

In QFT, calculating Vacuum Energy requires summing zero-point energies. One hypothesis is that the envelope \(g(u)\) acts as a natural regulator in operator \(L\).

However, this remains a hypothesis until we prove that the high-energy spectral behavior of \(L\) results in convergence. The next step is to derive the high-energy behavior of the eigenvalues \(\lambda_n\), and then determine whether the sum is convergent or requires zeta regularization.

3. Spectral Zeta Function and Vacuum Energy

If its non-zero spectrum is positive and the zero-mode treatment is specified, the spectral zeta function is defined over the non-zero spectrum as:

\[ \zeta_{L,\ne0}(s) = \sum_{\lambda_n>0} \lambda_n^{-s} \]

Given appropriate analytic continuation, the zeta-regularized vacuum energy is defined as:

\[ E_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{reg}} = \frac{1}{2}\zeta_{L,\ne0}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) \]
Handling the Zero-Mode: The identity \(Lg=0\) verifies the existence of a zero solution to the differential equation on the interior domain. Since \(g\) decays rapidly at the first endpoint and approaches zero linearly at the second, it is a candidate for a normalizable eigenfunction. However, confirming that \(\lambda_0=0\) is an eigenvalue of operator \(L\) requires proving that \(g\) belongs to the domain of a specific self-adjoint realization.

If this is established, the zeta function is defined on the non-zero spectrum as shown above. If the existence of the zero mode is not proven, the zeta function must be defined on the spectrum established by the chosen self-adjoint realization, not by assuming a zero mode in advance. The non-negativity of the operator must be proven independently through the domain and boundary conditions or through an appropriate quadratic form.

4. Results and Scope of the Model

What has been achieved (Mathematical Production): In Frame 3, we proved that the asymmetric envelope removes the symmetry argument (yielding \(R_{13/12} \approx 0.60352\)). In Frame 4, we established that the envelope \(g(u)\) satisfies the exact differential identity \(Lg=0\) on the interior domain, opening the way to a supersymmetric factorization approach.

Physical Prediction (Conditional): If self-adjointness, boundary conditions, and the connection between the operator and a physical system are established, the spectral predictions can be tested. The \(R_{13/12}\) ratio requires documented derivation and assumptions in Frame 3 to serve as a quantitative prediction.

Required Next Steps:

  1. Building a Self-Adjoint Realization: Define an appropriate domain for operator \(L\), and verify that \(g\) belongs to it.
  2. Studying the Spectrum: Prove spectral properties (such as the existence of a lower bound) before moving to vacuum energy interpretation.
  3. Numerical Calculation: Compute the first set of positive eigenvalues \(\lambda_n\) numerically.
