Jabri Structural Theory
Theoretical Basis, Research Hypotheses, and Numerical Validation

Not understood until understood.

1. Model

Response matrix \(S \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 16}\) with

\[ r_{ij} = \sqrt{(x_i-x_{s,j})^2 + (z_i-z_{s,j})^2}, \qquad S_{ij} = \frac{e^{-\alpha r_{ij}}}{r_{ij}+\epsilon}. \]

Fixed: \(\alpha=0.8\), \(\epsilon=0.1\), \(X=[0,1]\), \(Z=[0,1]\). 16 sources on a \(4\times 4\) grid in \([0.1,0.9]\times[0.1,0.9]\). The kernel is called Green-like. It is not claimed to be the exact physical Green's function of a specified PDE unless that PDE is stated separately.

Effective rank at 1% threshold: \(\#\{i : \sigma_i > 0.01\,\sigma_{\max}\}\). Condition numbers: \(\kappa_{\mathrm{reduced}} = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_r\), \(\kappa_{\mathrm{full}} = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{16}\).

2. Theoretical Basis

2.1 Unique Linear Recovery

For \(|I|=16\), \(S_I \in \mathbb{R}^{16\times 16}\). Then \(\det(S_I) \ne 0 \iff \mathrm{rank}(S_I)=16\), and \(S_I q = y\) has a unique solution for every \(y\). This is general linear algebra. Computed values support it numerically: \(\sigma_{\min}=1.877629 > 0\).

2.2 Greedy Selection and Full-Rank Recovery

Proposition. Let \(V_k\) be the span of the \(k\) selected rows and \(P_{V_k}\) the orthogonal projection onto \(V_k\). Adding a row \(s_i\) increases rank by one iff \(\|(I-P_{V_k})s_i\|_2 > 0\).

Since the response space is \(\mathbb{R}^{16}\), rank cannot exceed 16. At \(k=16\), the selected space is \(\mathbb{R}^{16}\) and residuals are zero in exact arithmetic. Stopping at 16 is a consequence of dimension, not an arbitrary programming choice. This proves independence, not global optimality.

2.3 Singular Value Stability

Weyl: \(|\sigma_i(A+E)-\sigma_i(A)| \le \|E\|_2\). Full rank is preserved if \(\|E\|_2 < \sigma_{\min}(A)\). With \(\sigma_{\min}=1.877629\), this is the precise stability statement. Orthogonality loss is a separate diagnostic of reorthogonalization quality, not by itself a measure of perturbation of the response matrix.

3. Research Hypotheses

H1: Effect of Depth Distribution

Distribution along \(Z\) affects independence of response vectors, hence effective rank and singular values. Status: numerically supported. Clearest evidence: F vs D — same \(N=32\), same Chebyshev in \(X\), different \(Z\). F rank 12, D rank 16.

H2: Uniform Depth in This Model

With Chebyshev fixed in \(X\), replacing logarithmic nodes in \(Z\) by uniform nodes can restore full rank in D. Status: numerically supported. Evidence: F vs D. Restricted to tested kernel, constants, domain, grids. No claim that uniform depth is always better.

5. Greedy Selection: Two Distinct Criteria

Greedy-Residual

\[ i_k = \arg\max_{i \in R_k} \|(I-Q_kQ_k^T)s_i\|_2. \]

Row-pivoted Gram–Schmidt selection.

Greedy-SVD

\[ i_k = \arg\max_{i \in R_k} \sigma_{\min}^{+}(S_{I_k \cup \{i\}}), \]

where \(\sigma_{\min}^{+}\) is the smallest nonzero singular value of the rectangular matrix in intermediate stages. Code:

s = np.linalg.svd(S[sel + [i]], compute_uv=False)
smin = s[-1]

Before 16 rows, the matrix is rectangular, so s[-1] is the smallest computed singular value, not an artificial zero.

The two criteria are distinct. They are not two names for one algorithm.

7. Scope of Conclusions

Two distinct greedy selection criteria, Greedy-Residual and Greedy-SVD, independently produced the same 16-sensor subset in the tested configuration. The selected response matrix achieved full numerical rank, with a reported smallest singular value of 1.877629 and condition number of 11.7293. This agreement provides numerical cross-validation of the selected subset, but does not establish global optimality.

Global optimality, \(\min_{|I|=16}\kappa_2(S_I)\), is not proved. Generalization outside the tested kernel, constants, domain, and grids is not claimed.
